The year, in four checks

What to look for.

Southern California · General guidance
01
March – May

Spring: look closely.

As days warm, take a closer look around the home and garden.

  • Check for ant trails and drainage problems.
  • Look for shed wings near windows and doors.
  • Some subterranean termites swarm on warm days after rain. Save photos for professional identification.

02
June – August

Summer: manage food and moisture.

Heat and dry spells can change where pests look for water and food.

  • Check kitchens, utility rooms, and outdoor bins for ant or cockroach activity.
  • Clean spills, seal food, and repair leaks.
  • Keep waste containers clean and secured.
Questions about pest signs? Call (213) 878-0161 ↗
03
September – November

Fall: inspect entry points.

A few simple checks can help identify openings and signs around your building.

  • Inspect door sweeps, screens, and utility openings.
  • Watch for drywood termite swarmers or shed wings in late summer and fall.
  • Secure stored food and reduce clutter near buildings.

04
December – February

Winter: keep checking indoors.

Cooler weather doesn’t mean pests disappear—especially inside.

  • After rain, check drainage and leaks around the building.
  • Monitor indoor storage and plumbing areas for rodent and cockroach signs.
  • Mild winters do not rule out pest activity.

Do you provide inspections or treatments?

No. Survival Pest Control is an independent information and referral website. We do not inspect properties, provide treatment quotes or perform pest treatments.

Does my San Gabriel Valley address guarantee service?

No. Confirm your exact ZIP code, pest, property type and appointment availability when you call. Coverage and eligibility depend on the provider.

Does pest activity follow a fixed seasonal schedule?

No. This calendar is a broad Southern California planning aid, not a prediction for an individual property. Indoor pests can occur year-round, and activity depends on weather, building conditions, and species.

What should I do if I see a termite swarmer?

Take a clear photo if you can do so safely, note where and when you saw it, and contact a pest professional for identification. A single sign alone may not establish the cause or extent of activity.

How can I make a pest call more useful?

Be ready to share your location, the areas where you’ve noticed activity, when it began, and any photos. Ask about inspection steps and treatment options for your particular concern.

Should I approach a bee or wasp nest?

No. Keep a safe distance and avoid disturbing it. Contact a qualified professional for guidance, especially if someone nearby has a known sting allergy.
