Bees and wasps: identify the concern.

Seeing insects around flowers is different from discovering a nest beside a doorway. Start by noting where activity occurs and keep a safe distance.

What are you seeing?

Honey bees often look fuzzy, while many wasps have a smoother appearance and a narrow waist. A hanging cluster of bees may be a temporary swarm; repeated flights into a wall or roof opening can indicate an established colony. Identification should come before removal decisions.

Keep your distance

Avoid disturbing nests, swatting insects, or blocking an active entrance. Keep people and pets away from concentrated activity. Close outdoor food containers and secure garbage lids.

Ask the right professional

For a bee swarm or established colony, ask whether a beekeeper or bee-removal specialist can assess it and whether live removal is appropriate. For a wasp nest near people, ask a qualified pest professional about identification and options.

Before you contact someone

  • Note the location, approximate height, and whether insects enter a structure.
  • Take photos only from a safe distance; do not approach a nest for a better picture.
  • Ask about removal methods, access, fees, and any cleanup or building repairs.
